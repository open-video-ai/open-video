# Open vs Closed — the viral benchmark series

> The same prompt. Two tools. One is **100% open-source, free, and runs on a single GPU**. The other
> is a closed API. Viewers guess which is which — then we reveal, with judge scores and Arena data.
>
> This is the highest-leverage content format in the entire calendar. It is the series that makes
> the project's thesis — **open video has closed the quality gap** — *visibly, repeatedly, and
> honestly true or not*. One rigged test kills it; one honest loss grows it. Credibility is the moat.
>
> **Audience:** the AI-video-curious public (X, Reddit, YouTube, HN). **Voice:** the same as the
> rest of the project — direct, evidence-first, no hype. **Canonical location of the living series
> page:** `open-video.ai/bench`. This doc is the editorial + production bible.

---

## 1. The concept — why this format goes viral

**The mechanic, in one line:** run the **same prompt** on **OpenVideo + H3** (open) and a **closed
leader** (Seedance / Sora / Veo / Kling), present the two clips **side-by-side with no labels**,
let viewers **guess which is open**, then **reveal** with the vision-judge scores, the Arena Elo
delta, and the full reproducible receipt.

It is engineered to do four jobs at once — and every job is a growth lever no closed competitor
can copy without undermining their own product:

1. **It makes the thesis visible.** "Open has caught closed" is a claim. *Same prompt, two clips,
   you literally cannot tell* is a demonstration. The AI-video community is primed to want this to
   be true; the format lets them *see* it, decide for themselves, and share the verdict.
2. **The "guess" is a built-in engagement trap.** A poll in the first half, the answer in the
   second. That is the platform-native shape of a viral unit (comment to vote, return for the
   reveal). It manufactures the two-visit loop that algorithms reward.
3. **It positions OpenVideo as the standard open reference.** Every episode names a closed
   competitor. Over a season, OpenVideo becomes *the* thing you compare closed tools to — the way
   Stable Diffusion became the open reference for image gen. Naming the incumbent is the SEO and
   curiosity lever, and it is true.
4. **The answer is a story the community wants to spread.** When the open clip is the good clip,
   the reply is *"and it's free and local."* When the open clip loses, the reply is *"and they
   posted the loss and filed the gap as an issue."* **Both outcomes build trust** — and trust is
   the only durable growth asset an open project has.

**The iron honesty rule (this is what makes the format defensible).** We never rig a test. We post
losses loudly. The closed competitor is always given the **same prompt, fairly, with no
OpenVideo-favoring tricks** (no prompt the closed tool can't handle, no hidden best-of-N on our
side that we don't disclose). The receipt — full prompt, seeds, settings, judge scores — ships with
every episode. **One rigged test kills the series' credibility forever. One honest loss earns a
hundred subscribers.** This is stated three times in this doc because it is the entire foundation.

**Why open can run this and closed can't.** Closed vendors will never publish a blind test where
they might visibly lose to a free alternative — it's brand suicide. OpenVideo can, because an
honest loss is an issue filed and a roadmap item, not a revenue threat. The format is structurally
ours.

## 2. The first comparison — "The Last Lighthouse" (OpenVideo) vs Seedance

This is **Episode 0** of the series. It uses the **actual demo film** already shipped
(`website/demo.mp4`, the landing-page hero) on the open side, and a **fair, research-based
characterization of what Seedance 2.x would produce on the same prompt** on the closed side.

> **Provenance and honesty, up front.** The OpenVideo side of this episode is **real** — the clip
> exists, the prompt is the exact one used, the numbers are from `ffprobe`, and the generation
> method is logged in the receipt (`55-ai-video/artifacts/verify/demo_film-2026-08-06.md`,
> ported here in §2.3). The Seedance side is a **characterization from competitive research**
> (`PLAN.md` competitive map + `docs/model-comparison.md` + `docs/POSITIONING.md`), **not a real
> generation we ran** — we do not have Seedance API access in scope for this episode. It is labeled
> as such everywhere it appears. The moment we have Seedance access, Episode 0 is re-cut with a
> **real** Seedance render on the identical prompt and the "(characterized, not generated)" tag is
> removed. **No claim is made that we ran Seedance.** This is the integrity standard for every
> episode: closed side is either *generated by us on the real tool* or *clearly marked as
> characterized*.

### 2.1 The concept clip (identical to both sides)

**Title:** "The Last Lighthouse"
**Logline:** A solitary lighthouse keeper stands against a storm at dusk; the beam sweeps, the sea
rages, a single lantern holds.

This is the demo film's concept — chosen because it stress-tests three quality axes at once:
**cinematic atmosphere** (golden-hour-to-storm lighting), **audio** (wind, waves, the rhythmic
lighthouse mechanism, diegetic thunder), and **multi-shot coherence** (one continuous keeper
across two shots).

### 2.2 The prompt (the actual 3-field H3 prompt used on the open side)

This is the literal prompt from `55-ai-video/prompts/demo/`. It is two shots because H3 caps at
~15 s/shot and the demo chains a T2V shot into an I2V shot (the FL2VA handoff — shot 2's first
frame = shot 1's last frame). **For the closed side, the same concept is given as a single
free-text prompt** (no shot-splitting), because that is how a creator would actually use Seedance.

**OpenVideo side — Shot 1 (T2VA, 10 s, seed 100):**
```
integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] Live-action, cinematic, a sweeping aerial shot
descends toward a solitary lighthouse on a rugged cliff at dusk, the last rays of golden light
catching the white tower against a deep blue stormy sea. The camera cranes down with large
amplitude at slow speed from the clouds to the lighthouse base, where an elderly keeper in a
weathered coat stands at the door, looking out at the horizon.

overall_soundscape: Wind howls across the cliff edge, waves crash against the rocks below, and
the lighthouse mechanism begins its rhythmic mechanical rotation with a deep metallic groan.

non_diegetic_music: A solitary cello sustains a low, mournful note, joined gradually by distant
string swells at a slow, elegiac tempo.
```

**OpenVideo side — Shot 2 (I2VA / FL2VA chain, 8 s, seed 200, first_frame = shot 1 last frame):**
```
integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] Live-action, cinematic, the elderly lighthouse
keeper in his weathered coat stands at the cliff edge as established, the lighthouse beam now
sweeping past him in steady rotations. The camera tracks right with small amplitude at slow
speed along the cliff edge, revealing the vast stormy sea below, lightning flickering on the
horizon, as the keeper raises a lantern and the lighthouse beam crosses with its warm glow.

overall_soundscape: The wind intensifies, the lighthouse mechanism groans with each rotation,
waves crash louder, and a distant rumble of thunder rolls in from the sea.

non_diegetic_music: The cello deepens, a low brass note enters underneath, building slightly as
the lightning flickers, then settling back to the sustained cello.
```

**Closed side (Seedance) — the same concept as a creator would type it:**
```
A cinematic live-action short: an elderly lighthouse keeper in a weathered coat stands alone on
a rugged cliff at dusk as a storm rolls in. A sweeping aerial shot descends from the clouds to
the lighthouse, then the camera tracks along the cliff edge revealing the stormy sea, lightning
on the horizon, and the keeper raising a lantern as the lighthouse beam sweeps past him. Wind
howls, waves crash, the lighthouse mechanism groans, a distant rumble of thunder. A mournful
cello score underlies the scene.
```

> The closed prompt is **deliberately the natural single-prompt version** a creator would write —
> this is the fair comparison. We do not handicap the closed tool by feeding it H3's shot-split
> 3-field grammar; we do not handicap OpenVideo by forcing a single shot it can't natively do. Each
> tool gets the prompt its own product is designed for, on the **same concept**.

### 2.3 The open side — what OpenVideo actually produced (real, verified)

Source: `55-ai-video/artifacts/verify/demo_film-2026-08-06.md` (the receipt), clip at
`website/demo.mp4`. Numbers from `ffprobe`:

| Axis | What the open clip shows | Real number |
|---|---|---|
| **Duration** | 2 chained shots (10 s T2V → 8 s I2V) | **18.16 s** total |
| **Resolution** | H3 native short-edge 768 px, 16:9 | **1344×768 @ 24 fps** |
| **Audio** | Native H3 stereo, diegetic + score | **AAC 32 kHz stereo** |
| **Coherence method** | FL2VA chain: shot 2 generated from shot 1's extracted last frame | verified in receipt |
| **Cost** | 1× RTX 5090, ~local minutes, $0 API | single consumer GPU |
| **Stack** | ComfyUI + H3 `pruned_int8_convrot`, sage attention, 20 steps | logged |
| **License** | Apache 2.0 (code) + open weights (model) | fully open + free |

**What it visibly does well (the strengths to lead with):** genuine cinematic atmosphere (the
golden-to-storm grade reads), coherent native audio (wind/waves/mechanism + cello score, no
TTS-dubbed tell), a real multi-shot handoff (the keeper is continuous across the cut), and it cost
nothing on hardware a hobbyist owns.

**What it visibly does *not* do well (the honest gaps to post alongside):** the FL2VA stitch is
not a native single generation — watch the cut and there is a subtle continuity seam a model-native
generation would not have; wide shots are H3's known weak point (`h3_ecosystem.md`: faces can
corrupt at distance), so the keeper at range is softer than a close-up would be; 18 s is short, and
the storm's peak physics (spray, lightning illumination of the waves) is implied more than
simulated at full fidelity.

### 2.4 The closed side — what Seedance 2.x would produce (characterized, not generated)

> **Tag: characterized from competitive research, not a generation we ran.** Grounded in
> `PLAN.md` ("Seedance does MODEL-natively a 180 s single generation"), `docs/model-comparison.md`
> (Seedance I2V Arena Elo ~1197), and `docs/POSITIONING.md` (Seedance ships a closed agentic
> long-video pipeline). Remove this tag once a real Seedance render is in hand.

On the identical lighthouse concept, **Seedance 2.x** would be expected to produce:

| Axis | What Seedance would produce (characterized) | Why / source |
|---|---|---|
| **Duration** | A **single native generation** of the full beat — no shot split, no stitch | Seedance is **model-native long**: up to ~180 s in one generation (`PLAN.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`) |
| **Resolution** | **~720p** model-native output | Seedance 2.x ships 720p (per task competitive research); upscales downstream |
| **Coherence** | **Native single-shot coherence** — no FL2VA seam, the keeper, beam, and storm are one continuous physical simulation | This is the structural advantage of model-native long generation over stitched orchestration (`POSITIONING.md`: "stitched coherence is weaker than a single native long generation") |
| **Audio** | Native or tightly integrated audio, but gated to Seedance's supported modes/languages | Closed audio is polished but constrained to whatever the API exposes |
| **Physics & lighting** | High-fidelity storm physics — spray, lightning illumination, wave churning — simulated in-model at competitive quality | Seedance sits at I2V Elo ~1197, within Arena noise of H3's ~1189 (`model-comparison.md`) |
| **Cost / access** | **Paid API, region-restricted, watermarked, rate-limited, ToS-governed** | This is the *only* axis where open wins structurally, every episode |

**The honest head-to-head verdict for Episode 0 (characterized):** on pure **visual + coherence
quality at this 18-second scale**, Seedance's model-native single generation likely edges the
stitched H3 clip — the seam-free coherence and simulated storm physics are exactly where native
long generation beats orchestration. **On audio fidelity, cost, freedom, and reproducibility**,
OpenVideo wins outright ($0, Apache 2.0, runs offline, fully reproducible from the receipt). The
Arena Elo gap (H3 ~1189 vs Seedance ~1197 I2V) is within benchmark noise — **the quality gap is
small enough that the blind vote is the honest test, and Episode 0 openly predicts a near-tie with
Seedance slightly ahead on coherence, OpenVideo far ahead on freedom.**

> **How Episode 0 is published given the closed side is characterized:** the blind "guess" frame is
> **not** used for Episode 0 (you cannot run a fair blind test without a real closed render).
> Instead Episode 0 ships as the **format premiere / series trailer**: the real OpenVideo clip +
> the honest characterized Seedance side-by-side, framed as *"here is the open clip; here is what
> the closed leader does on the same prompt; here is why we think the gap is now within noise on
> quality and decisive on freedom — and here is the format that will prove it, for real, every
> week, starting next week."* Episode 1 is the first **real blind** test (both sides generated).

## 3. The first season — 10 planned comparison prompts

Each episode isolates **one quality axis** so the series cumulatively maps the entire quality
surface (and OpenVideo's wins and losses are each explained, not vibes). The closed competitor
**rotates** (Seedance / Sora / Veo / Kling) so no single incumbent is harassed and SEO spreads
across all four names. Every prompt is **fair**: same concept to both tools, each in the form its
product expects.

> Format key: **Axis** = the quality dimension isolated · **Difficulty** = how hard for open today ·
> **Open lean** = where we honestly expect open to land (win / tie / lose) before the blind vote.

### Episode 1 — Cinematic / prompt-adherence (the first real blind test)
- **Closed competitor:** **Sora**
- **Axis:** adherence to a complex, multi-element cinematic brief (camera move + lighting + multiple subjects + a specified mood).
- **Difficulty:** medium. H3's 3-field grammar is built for this; this is OpenVideo's home turf.
- **Open lean:** **win or tie.** Prompt adherence is H3's stated strength (`model-comparison.md`).
- **Draft prompt concept:** *"A slow dolly-in on a rain-soaked Tokyo alley at 3 a.m.; neon reflections in the puddles, a lone figure under a transparent umbrella turning to look directly at camera, steam rising from a manhole cover, anamorphic lens flare, muted teal-and-amber grade, 35 mm film grain, melancholic."* (Adherence test: did all 7 elements survive?)

### Episode 2 — Product / macro-physics
- **Closed competitor:** **Veo**
- **Axis:** product-shot macro fidelity — materials, lighting, turntable motion, micro-reflections.
- **Difficulty:** hard. Product macro punishes any material/lighting weakness.
- **Open lean:** **tie or slight loss.** Pure aesthetic macro is where closed labs still invest heavily.
- **Draft prompt concept:** *"Studio product shot: a perfume bottle of frosted glass with a brushed-gold cap, rotating slowly on a seamless white cyclorama, a single soft key light raking across to reveal the engraving, shallow depth of field, 100 mm macro, photoreal."*

### Episode 3 — Dialogue / audio sync (FL2VA vs TTS-dubbed)
- **Closed competitor:** **Seedance**
- **Axis:** native audio + lip-synced dialogue. **The axis OpenVideo is structurally built to win** — H3 has native stereo audio; many closed pipelines dub TTS over a silent render.
- **Difficulty:** medium (for open, this is the advantage, not the cost).
- **Open lean:** **win.** This is the episode designed to be the series' flagship open victory.
- **Draft prompt concept:** *"Close-up on a woman at a dimly lit diner booth, rain on the window behind her, she looks up and says softly: <d>[English] 'I told you it wouldn't last.'</d>, a jukebox playing faintly in the background, diegetic rain and traffic, no music."* (The tell to watch: do the lips match the line, and is the ambience native or pasted?)

### Episode 4 — VFX / simulation (physics stress test)
- **Closed competitor:** **Kling**
- **Axis:** simulated physics — liquid, fire, particles, destruction. The classic "closed labs flex" axis.
- **Difficulty:** very hard. Physics simulation is an arms race closed labs lead.
- **Open lean:** **likely loss — and we say so up front.** File the gap as an issue; route future Wan 2.2 backend (`PLAN.md`: "Wan = physics/motion anchor") to close it.
- **Draft prompt concept:** *"A slow-motion shot of a crystal wine glass shattering on a black marble floor; the wine arcs outward in a perfect parabola, glass shards catch a single shaft of hard light, 1000 fps look, photoreal."*

### Episode 5 — Long-form coherence (the 60 s+ short) — OpenVideo's specialty
- **Closed competitor:** **Seedance** (the model-native long leader)
- **Axis:** coherence across a 60–90 s multi-shot film. **The thesis test.** No single open model does >15–30 s; only OpenVideo's agent layer (plan → FL2VA chain → judge → stitch) makes an open 60 s film possible at all.
- **Difficulty:** high ambition, fair fight. Seedance does this model-natively; OpenVideo does it by orchestration.
- **Open lean:** **the marquee episode.** Even a tie here is a headline: *"open orchestration matches model-native long generation at 60 s."* An honest loss is still a story (stitched drift vs native coherence) and a roadmap item.
- **Draft prompt concept:** a 4-shot, 60 s micro-thriller — *"A courier realizes the package she's carrying is counting down. One location, one character, escalating tension, a single twist."* (Tests: character continuity across 4 shots, prop continuity, story coherence — exactly what the coherence-bible + FL2VA chain are built for.)

### Episode 6 — Style lock / identity consistency
- **Closed competitor:** **Sora**
- **Axis:** one character, visually identical across multiple shots and angles (the reference-pack test).
- **Difficulty:** hard for open without a reference-pack; the whole point is to show OpenVideo's reference-pack workflow closing the gap closed tools handle internally.
- **Open lean:** **tie if the reference-pack lands; loss if it doesn't.** Honesty depends on whether the LoRA/ref-pack is ready.
- **Draft prompt concept:** *"The same woman — red coat, short dark hair, a small scar over her left eyebrow — in three shots: a wide on a train platform, a medium at a café window, a close-up turning to camera. Same person, unmistakably, across all three."*

### Episode 7 — Motion / camera (fast move + hands)
- **Closed competitor:** **Veo**
- **Axis:** fast camera motion and hand articulation — the classic deformity trap (limb melt, finger corruption, motion smear).
- **Difficulty:** hard. Fast motion + hands is where every model, open and closed, breaks; the test is who breaks *less*.
- **Open lean:** **unknown / honest coin-flip.** This is a genuine "we don't know, that's why we test" episode — the most credible kind.
- **Draft prompt concept:** *"A handheld chase shot following a street magician's hands as he shuffles a deck of cards at chest height, the camera orbiting him quickly, the cards flashing between his fingers, urban rooftop at golden hour."*

### Episode 8 — Human micro-detail (faces + skin at range)
- **Closed competitor:** **Kling**
- **Axis:** faces and skin detail — **H3's known weak point** (`h3_ecosystem.md`: wide shots can corrupt faces). This episode **deliberately picks a fight open might lose**, which is exactly why the audience will believe the wins.
- **Difficulty:** hard for open; we are going into this expecting a loss.
- **Open lean:** **likely loss.** The honest framing: *"we picked our model's known weak spot. Here's where open is today. Here's the issue."*
- **Draft prompt concept:** *"A wide group shot of five people at a dinner table, mid-conversation, candlelit, every face legible and expressive at distance, natural micro-expressions, 50 mm, photoreal."*

### Episode 9 — Aesthetic / cinematography (pure taste)
- **Closed competitor:** **Sora**
- **Axis:** pure cinematographic taste — lighting design, color, composition, film grammar. No physics, no dialogue, no coherence tricks. Just "does it look like a film?"
- **Difficulty:** subjective; this is the episode where the **blind community vote is the whole point** and judge scores are most debatable.
- **Open lean:** **tie.** Taste is where arena-parity open models genuinely compete.
- **Draft prompt concept:** *"A lone figure walks away down an empty boardwalk at dawn, fog diffusing the light, the silhouette small in frame, a long lens compressing the distance, muted desaturated grade, the shot held for the full duration with no cuts — Malick-meets-Kaurismäki."*

### Episode 10 — Audio-dominant scene (diegetic soundscape)
- **Closed competitor:** **Seedance**
- **Axis:** a scene where **audio is the subject** — the soundscape carries the meaning, not the image. Pairs with Episode 3 to make the dual case that native audio is OpenVideo's structural edge.
- **Difficulty:** medium; again this is the advantage axis, not the cost axis.
- **Open lean:** **win.**
- **Draft prompt concept:** *"POV: sitting in an empty cathedral during a thunderstorm. Rain drums on the high windows, distant thunder rolls and reverberates through the stone, a wooden door creaks open off-screen, footsteps echo and stop, a single drop hits a puddle in extreme close-up. The image is a static wide of the nave; the sound tells the whole story."*

## 4. Benchmark methodology — the integrity backbone

The series is only worth running if the method is unimpeachable. This is the protocol for every
episode. **Deviations from this protocol must be disclosed in the receipt or the episode does not
ship.**

### 4.1 The six controls (held identical on both sides)

1. **Same prompt concept.** The creative intent is identical. Each tool receives it **in the form
   its own product expects** (OpenVideo gets H3's 3-field shot-split grammar; the closed tool gets
   the natural single-prompt version). The exact text given to each is published in the receipt.
2. **Same target duration.** Both clips are produced at the same runtime (±10%). If a closed tool
   caps shorter, that is a **fair disclosed constraint**, not a handicap — the receipt says so.
3. **Same aspect ratio.** Both clips are the same frame shape. Letterboxing/pillarboxing to match
   is disclosed and minimized.
4. **No undisclosed best-of-N.** The default is **one generation per side, single seed**. If
   best-of-N is used on either side, **N is disclosed** and the *selected* clip + the *rejected*
   clips are all shown. (OpenVideo's judge→refine loop is allowed and **disclosed as the loop** —
   it is the product, not a test trick; the number of refine iterations is logged.)
5. **No post-production on either side** beyond what each tool's own product pipeline does
   natively (OpenVideo's stitcher + optional 2K upscale counts as native; manual color correction
   in Resolve does not). Any post is disclosed.
6. **Same publish path.** Both clips are encoded with the same settings for the blind version so
   encoder artifacts don't leak the answer.

### 4.2 The blind A/B (the engagement unit)

- **Two clips, side by side, labels randomized (A/B swapped per viewer where the platform allows;
  50/50 left-right otherwise).** No watermarks, no resolution tells, no UI chrome from either tool.
- **A poll:** "Which is the 100% open-source one? A or B?" The poll is the engagement engine.
- **24-hour vote window** before the reveal. Quotes from the vote are screenshotted (with
  permission/attribution) for the reveal post — "you guessed X, here's what it actually was."

### 4.3 The reveal + the receipt (the credibility unit)

The reveal ships with, **non-optionally**:

1. **The answer.** Which side was open, which was closed.
2. **The vote split.** What % guessed correctly. (A near-50/50 split is *itself* the thesis proven:
   the clips are indistinguishable.)
3. **The vision-judge scores.** Both clips scored by **cx (GPT-5.6) and Opus 4.8** on the same
   published rubric (§4.4), writer ≠ reviewer. Both verdicts logged. This is the same
   cross-model-review gate the project uses for its own films (`rules/cross-model-review.md`).
4. **The Arena Elo delta.** e.g. *"H3 T2V Elo ~1238 vs Sora ~1244 — within benchmark noise"*
   (`model-comparison.md`; re-check Arena before each quote — Elo drifts).
5. **The full receipt link:** `open-video.ai/bench/<slug>` → the prompt text, seeds, settings,
   generation time, GPU, judge scores, and (open side) the ComfyUI workflow / `library/` PR.

### 4.4 The published rubric (the same for every episode, both sides)

Each clip scored 1–10 on five axes by each judge; the per-episode table is the average. The rubric
is **published at series launch** so the community can audit scores against it.

| Axis | What 10/10 looks like |
|---|---|
| **Prompt adherence** | Every element of the brief is present and correct. |
| **Visual coherence** | No deformities, no flicker, identity/prop continuity holds, no stitch seams (or, for open, the seam is invisible). |
| **Motion quality** | Plausible physics, clean camera moves, no limb/hand melt. |
| **Aesthetic / cinematography** | Composed, lit, and graded like a film — taste, not just fidelity. |
| **Audio fidelity** (when applicable) | Native, in-scene, lip-synced where there is dialogue; no TTS-dubbed tell. |

> **Audio caveat in the rubric:** when a closed tool produces *no* native audio, it is scored on
> the four visual axes only and the receipt says so explicitly — OpenVideo's native-audio win is
> stated as a structural advantage, not quietly slipped into a higher average.

### 4.5 Community vote (the participatory layer)

- The poll result and the judge scores are **both published**, even when they disagree. A
  disagreement (community says A, judges say B) is *content gold* — it sparks exactly the
  "what even is quality?" debate that drives comments.
- A running **series leaderboard** lives at `open-video.ai/bench`: OpenVideo's win/tie/loss record
  across the season, per axis. An honest sub-.500 record is more persuasive than a fake .900.

## 5. Content cadence — one "Open vs Closed" post per week

> **Cadence: one episode per week.** This is more aggressive than the bi-weekly cadence sketched in
> `docs/CONTENT_CALENDAR.md` (Pillar 2 / §3 weekly table). **Recommendation: update
> CONTENT_CALENDAR.md to weekly to match** — the format is the calendar's single highest-leverage
> pillar and a weekly rhythm is what grows a series (algorithms reward consistency; the audience
> expects "Open vs Closed Tuesday"). If the production team cannot sustain weekly, fall back to the
> existing bi-weekly plan explicitly; do not run cadence that isn't resourced. **Decide once, log it
> in CONTENT_CALENDAR.md, keep it.**

### 5.1 The weekly unit (recommended)

- **Slot: every Tuesday, 09:00 PT** (catches US morning + EU evening; aligns with the existing
 CONTENT_CALENDAR Tuesday tutorial slot, which moves to Thursday or merges with the Weekly Short).

### 5.3 Format variations (rotate to find what hits)

Maintain a **2-episode buffer** of finished episodes (clips cut, receipts written) so a slow eng

## 7. Integrity rules (read these before every episode)

These are non-negotiable. Breaking one breaks the series.

1. **Never rig a test.** Same concept, fair prompt form for each tool, no OpenVideo-favoring
   tricks. If a test would embarrass the closed tool by construction, it is not a test — redesign it.
2. **Post losses loudly.** An honest loss + the issue filed is worth more than a hidden win.
   Predicted losses (Episodes 4, 8) are scheduled *because* they are credible.
3. **The receipt ships with every episode.** No receipt, no episode. The receipt is the
   `/bench/<slug>` page with prompt, seeds, settings, generation time, GPU, and both judge scores.
4. **The closed side is either real or clearly tagged.** Either we ran the closed tool ourselves
   (preferred, from Episode 1 onward) or the episode is marked **"characterized, not generated"**
   everywhere it appears (Episode 0 only, until real access exists). **Never imply we ran a tool we
   didn't.**
5. **Both judges, every time.** cx (GPT-5.6) and Opus 4.8 score every clip on the published rubric,
   writer ≠ reviewer (`rules/cross-model-review.md`). One model's verdict alone is not a score.
6. **Re-check Arena Elo before quoting.** Elo drifts; the number in the doc may be stale by publish
   day. Pull the live Arena number for the reveal post.
7. **Name the closed competitor truthfully.** Use the tool we actually compared against. The
   competitive framing ("open answer to Seedance") is from `POSITIONING.md` and is accurate; per-
   episode naming must match what we actually ran.
8. **Disclose every deviation.** Best-of-N, post-production, shorter closed duration, missing
   closed audio — all disclosed in the receipt. The audience's trust survives disclosed caveats;
   it does not survive discovered ones.
